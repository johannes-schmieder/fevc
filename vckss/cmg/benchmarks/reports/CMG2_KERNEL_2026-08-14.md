# CMG2 local Mata kernel calibration

## Scope and status

This is development evidence for selecting the v1 graph-action kernel. It is
not an end-to-end speed claim, an SCC portability result, or a promotion gate.
The workload is a unit-weight path Laplacian, which stresses the common sparse
case but does not represent every worker--firm graph.

- Platform: Apple arm64, Darwin 25.5.0, 192 GiB physical memory
- Runtime: Stata/MP 18, 8 registered processors
- Source commit at start: `744ca8ed7271b791a721d1d05011d864d439801a`
- Timings: one warmed process per row; figures are totals over the stated
  repetitions
- Correctness: every compared action had maximum relative difference zero on
  the deterministic sine-valued batch

## Two-reduction versus directed arcs

The first prototype used two endpoint reductions. It stored the second
endpoint order and precomputed endpoint panels, bounded each temporary at
64 MiB, and evaluated every edge twice. The unbounded directed-arc prototype
stored two arcs per edge and evaluated one grouped reduction.

Prototype source SHA-256:

- core: `c1784b69293dfa61d8186198a8eead937c1b0aef532c03ca5fa32ef1d94ca5ec`
- benchmark: `9530daabccaed19bdf47835bdb98820066597be113ef5b3e2125c6b05c2dd8f0`

| Vertices | RHS columns | Repetitions | Two reduction (s) | Unbounded arcs (s) | Arc/two ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | 8 | 10 | 0.013 | 0.005 | 0.385 |
| 100,000 | 8 | 5 | 0.058 | 0.021 | 0.362 |
| 1,000,000 | 1 | 5 | 0.235 | 0.150 | 0.638 |
| 1,000,000 | 8 | 3 | 0.334 | 0.133 | 0.398 |
| 1,000,000 | 32 | 2 | 1.391 | 0.193 | 0.139 |

The unbounded prototype was decisively faster, especially for wider batches,
but its `2E x batch` temporary violates the registered 64 MiB scratch cap.

## Selected bounded directed-arc kernel

The selected kernel stores sorted arc edge/sign vectors and vertex panels,
then processes complete vertex groups in column and arc chunks. A single
exceptional hub group is reduced in consecutive partial chunks. Thus no
temporary scales with `2E x batch`, while the hot path retains one grouped
arc reduction rather than two endpoint reductions.

Selected source SHA-256:

- core: `7f977d39d0637dd29823e6cf32cd80630e71d9ad877bf3993fa10bc6e804450c`
- benchmark: `32c499021e2d5f4ec4ba8dbd302d9d8e1ab096ab18507e84beac5b8afc59a397`

| Vertices | RHS columns | Repetitions | Bounded arcs (s) | Unbounded arcs (s) | Unbounded/bounded |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000,000 | 1 | 5 | 0.201 | 0.149 | 0.741 |
| 1,000,000 | 8 | 3 | 0.271 | 0.133 | 0.491 |
| 1,000,000 | 32 | 2 | 0.875 | 0.203 | 0.232 |

At one million path edges the stored production graph estimate was
111,999,944 bytes. The separate unbounded prototype required another
63,999,952 bytes of arc metadata and then formed its unbounded value matrix.
The production estimate includes its own arc metadata and excludes Mata/OS
allocator overhead; it is not an RSS measurement.

## Decision and remaining gates

Freeze the bounded directed-arc action for Stata 18 in v1. It is 1.17--1.59
times faster than the bounded two-reduction prototype in the directly
comparable one-million-vertex rows, while respecting the scratch contract.
The decision remains provisional for Stata 19 and for hub-heavy, dense,
disconnected, and highly unbalanced weighted graphs. CMG10 must repeat the
kernel and end-to-end gates on SCC before portability or production claims.
