\noindent The note treats the control coefficients as known. This addendum gives the variance of the fixed-offset
estimator when they are estimated, an estimator of that variance that fits the repeat-measurement method, the
$q=1$ pieces, and a numerical check against the exact variance. It separates what is exact, what holds under stated
conditions, and what is an approximation that has only been checked numerically. Notation follows Kline, Saggio and
S{\o}lvsten (2020, KSS) and the note.

\section{Setup}
Physical observation $\tau=1,\dots,F_i$ of deletion unit $i=1,\dots,n$ belongs to worker $w(i)$ and firm $f(i)$:
$y_{i\tau}=\alpha_{w(i)}+\psi_{f(i)}+z_{i\tau}'\gamma+\varepsilon_{i\tau}$, $z_{i\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^k$. Errors have mean
zero and are independent across units; within a unit they may be dependent. Under match deletion a unit is a
mover's match ($F_i\ge1$) or one observation of a stayer; under observation deletion every observation is a unit.
Unit $i$ is one row,
\[
 x_i=\sqrt{F_i}\,(e_{w(i)}+e_{f(i)}),\qquad y_i(g)=F_i^{-1/2}\sum_\tau\bigl(y_{i\tau}-z_{i\tau}'g\bigr),\qquad
 \varepsilon_i=F_i^{-1/2}\sum_\tau\varepsilon_{i\tau},\qquad \sigma_i^2=\Var[\varepsilon_i],
\]
with $\mu_i=x_i'\beta$, $S_{xx}=\sum_ix_ix_i'$, $P=XS_{xx}^{-1}X'$, $M=I-P$, $B=XS_{xx}^{-1}AS_{xx}^{-1}X'$ for the
target $\theta=\beta'A\beta$, and the leave-out kernel $C_{i\ell}=B_{i\ell}-\tfrac12M_{i\ell}(B_{ii}/M_{ii}+
B_{\ell\ell}/M_{\ell\ell})$, $C_{ii}=0$. For a known coefficient $g$, $\tilde\theta(g)=y(g)'Cy(g)$ is the KSS estimator,
unbiased at $g=\gamma$.

$\hat\gamma$ is the coefficient of the full least-squares fit on worker dummies, firm dummies and the controls.
With $\tilde z_{i\tau}$ the controls after removing the two-way effects over physical observations and
$\Lambda=(\sum_{i,\tau}\tilde z_{i\tau}\tilde z_{i\tau}')^{-1}$, $\delta=\hat\gamma-\gamma=\Lambda\sum_{i,\tau}\tilde
z_{i\tau}\varepsilon_{i\tau}$. \textbf{The estimator studied here is the fixed-offset one,
$\hat\theta=\tilde\theta(\hat\gamma)$.} The command's default re-estimates $\gamma$ inside every leave-out fit; that
estimator is a different one (Section~\ref{sec:joint}).

Unit aggregates: $Z_i=F_i^{-1/2}\sum_\tau z_{i\tau}$, $\tilde Z_i=F_i^{-1/2}\sum_\tau\tilde z_{i\tau}$,
$\bar z_i=F_i^{-1}\sum_\tau z_{i\tau}$, $\omega_i=\sum_\tau(z_{i\tau}-\bar z_i)\varepsilon_{i\tau}$,
$G_i=\sum_\tau(z_{i\tau}-\bar z_i)(z_{i\tau}-\bar z_i)'$; $Z,\tilde Z$ are $n\times k$. Since the two-way part of $z$
is constant within a unit, $\delta=\Lambda(\tilde Z'\varepsilon+\omega)$ with $\omega=\sum_i\omega_i$. Write
$\kappa_i=\Cov[\varepsilon_i,\omega_i]$, $\Omega_i=\Var[\omega_i]$, $K$ for the $n\times k$ matrix with rows $\kappa_i'$,
$\Omega=\sum_i\Omega_i$, $V=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i^2)$ and $\Pi=I-\tilde Z\Lambda Z'$. For $F_i=1$,
$\omega_i=0$.

\section{The variance of the fixed-offset estimator}
\paragraph{The noise of the adjusted outcome.} $y(\hat\gamma)=\mu+v$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v}
 v=\varepsilon-Z\delta=\Pi'\varepsilon-Z\Lambda\omega,\qquad
 S=\Var[v]=\Pi'V\Pi-\Pi'K\Lambda Z'-Z\Lambda K'\Pi+Z\Lambda\Omega\Lambda Z'.
\end{equation}
$S$ equals $V$ plus a symmetric matrix of rank at most $2k$. The estimator is the quadratic form
$\hat\theta=(\mu+v)'C(\mu+v)$, so with $m=C\mu$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact}
 \hat\theta-\theta=2m'v+v'Cv,\qquad \E\hat\theta-\theta=\tr(CS),\qquad
 \Var[\hat\theta]=4m'Sm+2\tr(CSCS)\ \text{ for Gaussian errors.}
\end{equation}
These are exact. (\emph{Proved.}) The bias $\tr(CS)$ can be nonzero because $S$ is not diagonal.

\paragraph{The linear part.} $2m'v=\sum_i(2m_i^\ast\varepsilon_i+c'\omega_i)$ with $m^\ast=\Pi m$, $c=-2\Lambda Z'm$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Vlin}
 4m'Sm=\sum_i\bigl(4m_i^{\ast2}\sigma_i^2+4m_i^\ast\kappa_i'c+c'\Omega_ic\bigr).
\end{equation}
With known controls it is $4\sum_im_i^2\sigma_i^2$. (\emph{Proved}; for two targets
$4m_s'Sm_t$.) The linear part is exactly uncorrelated with $\varepsilon'C\varepsilon$, without Gaussianity: since
$C_{ii}=0$, each term of $\varepsilon'C\varepsilon$ involves two different units, and multiplying it by the linear
contribution of any unit leaves an independent factor with mean zero. This does not establish zero covariance with
$v'Cv$ for non-Gaussian errors.

\paragraph{The quadratic part.} $v'Cv=\varepsilon'C\varepsilon+R$ with
$R=-2\varepsilon'CZ\delta+\delta'Z'CZ\delta$, a quadratic form of rank at most $2k$ in the physical errors. Low rank
does not make it small: writing $V_0=4m'Sm+2\tr(CVCV)$, what is needed is
$\Var[R]=o(V_0)$ and $\E R=o(\sqrt{V_0})$. \emph{These hold under stated conditions, not in general}. In the Gaussian case
it suffices that $k$ is fixed, the covariance of the physical errors has bounded operator norm,
$\|Z\Lambda^{1/2}\|_{\rm op}$ is bounded and $\|C\|_{\rm op}^2/V_0\to0$; then $\Var[R]=O(k\|C\|_{\rm op}^2)$ and
$|\E R|=O(k\|C\|_{\rm op})$. The conditions can fail when a dominant quadratic direction is aligned with the controls. (The reviewer's example: two workers,
one firm, $z$ equal to the worker indicator plus a within-worker contrast; $\max_i\tilde Z_i'\Lambda\tilde Z_i=1/n$,
yet $\Var[\hat\theta]/(2\tr(CVCV))\to4$ and the bias is of the order of the standard deviation.) Formula
\eqref{eq:exact} with $S$ does not need this paragraph or its conditions; this is why the estimator below is built on $S$ rather than
on $V$ plus a first-order term.

\subsection{The jointly estimated version}\label{sec:joint}
For observation units the joint estimator is $y'C^{\rm f}y$ with the kernel of the full design $[X,\mathbf Z]$.
$B^{\rm f}=\Pi B\Pi'$ exactly, so the $B$-part of its linear influence equals that of $m^\ast$. (\emph{Proved.}) The
leave-out corrections differ: with $D=\operatorname{diag}(B_{ii}/M_{ii})$, $M^{\rm f}=M-\tilde Z\Lambda\tilde Z'$ and
$D^{\rm f}=\operatorname{diag}(B^{\rm f}_{ii}/M^{\rm f}_{ii})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint}
 m_{\rm joint}-m^\ast=-\tfrac12M^{\rm f}\bigl[(D^{\rm f}-D)\mu+D^{\rm f}Z\gamma\bigr].
\end{equation}
The second term can be non-negligible. It depends on $M^{\rm f}D^{\rm f}Z\gamma$, so it vanishes when $D^{\rm f}$ is a
scalar matrix even if the control index $Z\gamma$ varies. It arises because the joint estimator uses the raw outcome in
its correction, so it is not invariant to adding $Za$ to the outcome, while the fixed-offset estimator is.
\textbf{The two estimators are therefore not first-order equivalent in general}, and the variance below describes
the fixed-offset estimator. (The reviewer's example: workers with two or four observations, a control alternating
$\pm1$, unit error variance: $\Var[\hat\theta_{\rm joint}]/\Var[\hat\theta_{\rm fixed}]\to1+4\gamma^2/45$.) In the
check of Section~\ref{sec:num} the joint variance is 0.995 to 1.035 times the
fixed-offset one. A term for \eqref{eq:joint} is not part of this addendum.

\section{Estimating it within the repeat-measurement method}
The method supplies unit variance estimates $\hat\sigma_i^2$ (the sample variance $q_U$ of the adjusted outcomes
of a repeat-measurement set $U$ for its units; a fitted value elsewhere), $\hat V=\operatorname{diag}(\hat\sigma_i^2)$,
the influence estimates $\hat m=Cy(\hat\gamma)$, and pseudo-noise draws. Let $\tilde e_i=\hat e_i/M_{ii}$ be the
leave-out residual of unit $i$ in the unit design and
$w_i=\sum_\tau(z_{i\tau}-\bar z_i)\bigl(y_{i\tau}-\bar y_i-(z_{i\tau}-\bar z_i)'\hat\gamma\bigr)$ the realised
within-unit product; $\hat c=-2\Lambda Z'\hat m$, $\hat m^\ast=\Pi\hat m$, $\hat\Omega=\sum_iw_iw_i'$.

\paragraph{Linear part.} The plug-in for $4m'Sm$ with realised products for $\kappa_i$ and $\Omega_i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lhat}
 \hat L=\sum_i\bigl(4\hat m_i^{\ast2}\hat\sigma_i^2+4\hat m_i^\ast\tilde e_i\,w_i'\hat c+(w_i'\hat c)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
With $S$ known, $\E[4\hat m'S\hat m]=4m'Sm+4\tr(CSCS)$ exactly, since $\Var[\hat m]=CSC$.

\paragraph{Quadratic part.} Draw $\zeta\sim N(0,\hat V)$ and independent standard normals $\xi_i$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:draw}
 v^\ast=\zeta-Z\Lambda\bigl(\tilde Z'\zeta+\textstyle\sum_iw_i\xi_i\bigr),\qquad
 \Var^\ast[v^\ast]=S_d=\Pi'\hat V\Pi+Z\Lambda\hat\Omega\Lambda Z' .
\end{equation}
With $u=Cv^\ast$, $\hat q$ is the average over draws of $u'S_du=\sum_i\hat\sigma_i^2(\Pi u)_i^2+
(\Lambda Z'u)'\hat\Omega(\Lambda Z'u)$, an estimate of $\tr(CS_dCS_d)$. The estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Vhat}
 \hat V[\hat\theta]=\hat L-2\hat q+\text{pair term},
\end{equation}
which without controls is the existing $4\sum_i\hat\sigma_i^2\hat m_i^2-2\widehat{\tr}(C\hat VC\hat V)+$ pair term.
With $S$ known and Gaussian errors, $4\hat m'S\hat m-2\tr(CSCS)$ is exactly unbiased for \eqref{eq:exact}.

\paragraph{What is approximate in the feasible version.} The following discrepancies remain; their aggregate effect
has not been bounded. The combined procedure is checked below in one Gaussian design (Section~\ref{sec:num}).
Bounded whole-unit leverage is weaker than the vanishing maximum influence usually needed to replace the moments by
these estimates. Every feasible claim is for Gaussian errors: with non-Gaussian physical errors the transformed
quadratic form can have nonzero diagonal blocks within units, so third- and fourth-cumulant contributions to its
variance are not identically zero.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item $S_d$ leaves out the $K$ terms of $S$; they are kept in $\hat L$ through the realised cross products, and the
draw is not changed. This does not compensate. Let $S_0=S+H$ be the covariance of the draw without $K$ when the
covariance inputs are known, $H=\Pi'K\Lambda Z'+Z\Lambda K'\Pi$, of rank at most $2k$. Even with the linear part
$4\hat m'S\hat m$, the trace based on $S_0$ leaves the discrepancy
$2\{\tr(CSCS)-\tr(CS_0CS_0)\}=-4\tr(CSCH)-2\tr(CHCH)$, whose sign is not controlled. Under bounded $\|S\|_{\rm op}$ and
$\|H\|_{\rm op}$ it is $O(k\|C\|_{\rm op}^2)$, negligible for fixed $k$ when $\|C\|_{\rm op}^2/\Var[\hat\theta]\to0$.
It can be first order with a dominant direction, even when the control information is diffuse: with $S=I$,
$S_0=I+dd'$ for a diffuse unit vector $d$ and a kernel approaching $\lambda dd'$, the two traces approach $\lambda^2$
and $4\lambda^2$. The method meets that case through Section~\ref{sec:q1}: when one direction carries more than ten
per cent of the estimated variance, the interval is the $q=1$ interval, whose leading-score variance
$\Sigma_{bb}=d'Sd$ is computed with the $K$ terms; the quadratic part enters only through the remainder kernel $C^r$,
to which the bound applies with $C^r$ in place of $C$. The row $\kappa_i$ of $K$ is zero when the errors of unit $i$
have one variance and are uncorrelated within the unit, because the within-unit control deviations sum to zero; the
discrepancy needs within-unit heteroskedasticity that moves with the controls, or serial correlation. An algebraic
repair is available and not implemented: with $\hat K_{i\cdot}=\tilde e_iw_i'$ and
$\hat H=\Pi'\hat K\Lambda Z'+Z\Lambda\hat K'\Pi$, use
$\hat q_{\rm full}=\hat q-2\tr(CS_dC\hat H)+\tr(C\hat HC\hat H)$, whose conditional mean is
$\tr\{C(S_d-\hat H)C(S_d-\hat H)\}$, while $\hat L=4\hat m'(S_d-\hat H)\hat m$ exactly. The middle term is the average of
$u'\hat Hu$ over the existing draws; the last needs $2k$ further applications of $C$, whereas the present
implementation needs no linear solves beyond those of the model without controls. $S_d-\hat H$ need not be positive
semidefinite, so it cannot be drawn from.
\item $w_i=\omega_i-G_i\delta$, not $\omega_i$: the realised product is shrunk by the fit, as a residual is. In the
extreme case of one match carrying all information on $\gamma$, $w_i=0$ while $\Omega_i>0$.
\item $\E[\tilde e_iw_i]=\kappa_i-\sigma^2G_i\Lambda\tilde Z_i/M_{ii}$ under homoskedastic independent errors: the
cross product is biased by a control-leverage term.
\item The sample variance of a set is computed from adjusted outcomes: under homoskedasticity
$\E q_U=\sigma^2[1-\tr(\Lambda Z_U'H_UZ_U)/(T_U-1)]$, $H_U=I-\mathbf{11}'/T_U$, and fitting $\gamma$ makes different
sets dependent. The pair term is kept unchanged; its exact justification (constant kernel entries inside a set,
independent sets) no longer applies.
\item The leave-out products used to fit the model for units outside sets have mean $(SM)_{ii}/M_{ii}$ rather than
$\sigma_i^2$.
\item Products of the estimated $\hat m^\ast,\hat c$ with the realised $w_i,\tilde e_i$ contain fourth-order
interactions that $\hat q$ does not remove.
\item One sample variance per set. For independent adjusted outcomes $Y_i$, $i\in U$, with equal means and the true
controls removed, $\E q_U=\bar\sigma_U^2=T_U^{-1}\sum_{i\in U}\sigma_i^2$. Pooling is then unbiased for the linear
term $\sum_{i\in U}a_i\sigma_i^2$, $a_i=4(m_i^\ast)^2$, exactly when
$\sum_{i\in U}(a_i-\bar a_U)(\sigma_i^2-\bar\sigma_U^2)=0$; with controls the weights $a_i$ vary inside a set. Zero
linear correlation of the variances with a control is not enough, because the weights are quadratic: control-index
values $(-1,0,1)$ and variances $(2,1,2)$ are linearly uncorrelated, yet with weights equal to the squared index the
weighted sum is $4$ and pooling gives $10/3$. Exact cancellation in each set is stronger than needed: the aggregate
discrepancy must be negligible relative to the variance. The condition covers the linear part only; the pair term
with $q_U^2$ also uses one variance per set. \emph{Not adopted:} for sets of three or more units,
$\hat v_i^\times=(T_U(Y_i-\bar Y_U)^2-q_U)/(T_U-2)$ is unbiased for $\sigma_i^2$ under these conditions without
Gaussianity, but it can be negative, so it cannot feed the draw; the pair term would need a new calculation; and with
fitted controls it is not exactly unbiased.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Weak identification (the author's finding, not the reviewer's).} Where the control coefficient is poorly
identified, or the sensitivity of the estimate to it is itself noisy in a small sample, the second-order noise
dominates and the unbiased variance estimate \eqref{eq:Vhat} can be non-positive. The command then reports the
component without a normal interval: the interval is withheld, or the $q=1$ interval is used. This was observed on a
test fixture with a control whose within-unit variation was small (amplitude 0.13); a well-identified control on the
same data gives agreement with a dense oracle to 0.004 per cent at 32{,}768 draws on the exact route.

\section{The $q=1$ pieces}\label{sec:q1}
For a component with leading direction $d$ (unit-space weights of unit length; a function of the design) and
eigenvalue $\lambda$, the note writes $\hat\theta=\lambda(\hat b^2-\hat V_{\rm lo}[\hat b])+\hat\theta_r$ with
$\hat b=d'y(\hat\gamma)$, $\hat V_{\rm lo}[\hat b]=\sum_id_i^2y_i(\hat\gamma)\tilde e_i$ and $\hat\theta_r$ the leave-out
form with the remainder kernel $C^r$; this is an identity. With estimated controls $\hat b-b=d'v$, so
\[
 \Sigma_{bb}=d'Sd=\sum_i\bigl((\Pi d)_i^2\sigma_i^2+2(\Pi d)_i\kappa_i'c_b+c_b'\Omega_ic_b\bigr),\qquad c_b=-\Lambda Z'd,
\]
exactly (\emph{proved}), and $\hat\theta_r-\theta_r=2m^{r\prime}v+v'C^rv$ gives
$\Cov[\hat b,2m^{r\prime}v]=2d'Sm^r$ and, for Gaussian errors, $\Var[\hat\theta_r]=4m^{r\prime}Sm^r+2\tr(C^rSC^rS)$,
$\Cov[\hat b,v'C^rv]=0$, $\E\hat\theta_r-\theta_r=\tr(C^rS)$. Estimates: \eqref{eq:Lhat} and \eqref{eq:Vhat} with
$C^r$ for $\Sigma_{rr}$ (the same draws); for $\Sigma_{bb}$ the larger of the fitted version
$\sum_i((\Pi d)_i^2\hat\sigma_i^2+2(\Pi d)_i\tilde e_iw_i'c_b+(w_i'c_b)^2)$ and the version with the leave-out
products $y_i\tilde e_i$ in place of $\hat\sigma_i^2$; for $\Sigma_{br}$ the plug-in for $2d'Sm^r$. A positive
definiteness check of the $2\times2$ matrix remains necessary: with realised products a fitted $\Sigma_{bb}$ need
not be positive.

\paragraph{Centring.} $\E\hat\theta-\theta=\lambda\{\Sigma_{bb}-\E\hat V_{\rm lo}\}+\tr(C^rS)$. With controls the first
term need not be small on the weak-direction scale: $\E\hat V_{\rm lo}=\sum_id_i^2(SM)_{ii}/M_{ii}$, which in general
differs from $d'Sd$. This is a bias of the \emph{point estimate}. The interval of KSS Section~7.1 is built from
$(\hat b,\hat\theta_r)$ and the map $\lambda b^2+\theta_r$; $\hat b$ is unbiased for $b$ and $\hat\theta_r$ for
$\theta_r$ up to $b_r=\tr(C^rS)$, so the interval need not inherit the first term, and the decomposition is left
unchanged. What it needs, and what is \emph{not proved} here, is joint normality of $(\hat b,\hat\theta_r)$ with the
control parts included (the Lindeberg condition is now on $(\Pi d)_i\varepsilon_i+c_b'\omega_i$, not on $d_i$ alone),
consistency of the three estimated entries, and a centring condition for the remainder: with $\rho$ the correlation
of $\hat b$ and $\hat\theta_r$, $|b_r|/\sqrt{\Sigma_{rr}(1-\rho^2)}\to0$ (if $|\rho|$ stays away from one,
$b_r=o(\sqrt{\Sigma_{rr}})$ suffices), or $b_r$ estimated and subtracted. Joint normality and a consistent covariance
do not alone give coverage when the expectation is displaced from the target. The interval is built on the
two-dimensional statistic $(\hat b,\hat\theta_r)$ and need not be centred on the reported scalar estimate, which may
keep a bias $\lambda\{\Sigma_{bb}-\E\hat V_{\rm lo}\}$; there is no inconsistency in that.

\section{Numerical check against the exact variance}\label{sec:num}
A dense calculation: 260 workers, 16 firms, 1{,}160 person-years, $k=2$ controls correlated with the effects and
varying within every match, Gaussian errors. The truth is \eqref{eq:exact}, computed exactly. Each estimator is
averaged over 1{,}500 outcome draws with exact traces; unit variances are the sample variance within every
repeat-measurement set and the true variance elsewhere (the fitted model is not under test). Entries: mean of the
estimate over the exact variance. ``Oracle'' is $4\hat m'S\hat m-2\tr(CSCS)$ with the true $S$ (a check of the
calculation); ``known'' treats the control coefficients as known.

``First draft'' is the estimator of the first version of this addendum (corrections with $V$ in place of $S$); in
this design it agrees with \eqref{eq:Vhat} to three digits, so the difference between them is not what the check
can show. The shortfall in the last row is consistent with item~7 of the list: under observation deletion every
worker--firm cell with two or more observations is a set, and here its variances move with the control inside the
cell; the control adjustment of the set variances and the unchanged pair term are also active in that row. The bias
of the point estimate, $\tr(CS)$, is 0.01 standard deviations. Under observation units the jointly estimated version
has 0.995 to 1.035 times the fixed-offset variance. The combined check uses true variances outside sets and exact
traces; it does not test the fitted model for units outside sets, and it concerns the fixed-offset estimator, not
the default jointly estimated one. Simulations through the command (default joint estimation, fitted model, random
draws) are reported separately in the package documentation (\texttt{fevc/docs/default\_interval/}), not here.

\paragraph{Status.} Proved: \eqref{eq:v}--\eqref{eq:Vlin}, $B^{\rm f}=\Pi B\Pi'$, \eqref{eq:joint}, $\Sigma_{bb}=d'Sd$,
the unbiasedness of the oracle form. Under stated conditions only: that $R$ is negligible (not needed by
\eqref{eq:Vhat}). Checked numerically only, in one Gaussian design: the feasible estimator \eqref{eq:Vhat} with its seven
approximations. Available, not implemented: the repair $\hat q_{\rm full}$ of item~1. Not covered: the joint
estimator's additional term \eqref{eq:joint}; asymptotic normality, consistency and the remainder centring condition
for the $q=1$ pieces with controls; coverage, which is for the simulations through the command, reported separately.
